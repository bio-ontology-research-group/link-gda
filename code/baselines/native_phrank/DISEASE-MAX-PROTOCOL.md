# Native HPO Phrank disease-max comparison, fixed 29 October 2026

The published Phrank gene score is the maximum phenotype similarity over diseases associated with the gene (Jagadeesh et al., Genetics in Medicine 21, 464–470, doi:10.1038/s41436-018-0072-y). The existing direct-gene arm uses the author's separate API for scoring the union of a gene's annotations. Retain both, with explicit names and raw scoring; do not choose between them using test performance. This additional comparison uses a benchmark already examined during development.

Use Phrank 1.6's disease-annotation constructor with only inner-training gene–disease pairs and their phenotype profiles. The author implementation derives gene annotations and conditional information content from these training diseases. For each test query, score each training disease and take the maximum for each linked candidate gene. No reference-self normalization or additional fitted parameter is used. Unknown HPO terms remain singletons, and candidates without training annotations receive score zero and average-rank ties. All 13,625 candidates and all ten frozen folds remain in the comparison. The HPO extraction, source inputs and split-isolation rules are identical to the direct-gene protocol.

Cache disease closures and sum marginal information content deterministically with `math.fsum`, as in the direct adapter. Before completion, compare sampled real-query gene scores against the author `rank_genes_using_disease` API at absolute/relative tolerance 1e-12. An integration fixture must distinguish a gene with two separate disease profiles from a gene with their union in one disease, verify both against the author API, and retain unsupported candidates. Record mode, protocol/source/input hashes, training disease annotations, candidate/query identities and raw/calibrated scores. Calibration remains a separately labelled sensitivity.

Write to a fresh output root distinct from direct-gene results. Preserve the original direct-gene protocol and retained results. This comparison tests the published disease-max scoring rule under the study's fold-specific whole-candidate setting; clinical validation still requires real patient phenotypes and patient-specific variant lists.
